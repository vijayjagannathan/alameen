title: "Freemasonry: Myths and Misconceptions"
date: 2026-08-02
author: Vijay Jagannathan
excerpt: Freemasonry is private, not secret — misconceptions persist regardless.
---
Few organisations have attracted as much speculation as Freemasonry. Films, novels and internet forums have built an entire mythology around the fraternity, most of it wrong. Separating what Freemasonry actually is from what fiction has made it out to be is worth doing plainly.

**Myth: Freemasonry is a secret society.**

Freemasonry is a private organisation, not a secret one. Its lodges are listed in public directories, its buildings carry visible signage, and its members are generally open about their membership. What is private is the content of its ceremonies, in the same way a professional body might keep its internal proceedings private. The distinction between private and secret matters, and it is the one most often lost in popular retellings.

**Myth: Freemasonry is a religion, or a substitute for one.**

Freemasonry requires belief in a Supreme Being but does not define that belief, does not conduct worship and does not offer a path to salvation. Members practise their own faiths outside the lodge. Christians, Muslims, Hindus, Sikhs, Parsis and Jews have sat in lodges together for generations, and religious debate is deliberately excluded from lodge business precisely so that this diversity can coexist.

**Myth: Freemasonry is a vehicle for political or business influence.**

Political discussion is barred from lodge meetings by long-standing rule, not by custom alone. Using membership to solicit business or favours from another member is considered a serious breach of Masonic conduct. The idea of a coordinated Masonic network working behind the scenes in government or commerce has no basis in how lodges actually function, which are small, locally governed bodies with no central directive over members’ professional lives.

**Myth: Freemasonry is linked to the occult or to secret world control.**

This claim, popularised by conspiracy literature over the past two centuries, has no connection to what happens inside a lodge. The rituals draw on the symbolism of stonemasons’ tools and on biblical and classical allegory common to Western moral education of the period the fraternity emerged in. There is no occult practice, no world governance agenda and no hidden hierarchy above the Grand Lodges that oversee the craft.

**Myth: Women cannot be involved in Freemasonry at all.**

Mainstream Craft Freemasonry, of the kind practised at Al-Ameen, is open to men. Separate Masonic and Masonic-adjacent organisations for women exist under different constitutions and traditions, and members’ families are often included in social and charitable events, but the two structures are distinct and not interchangeable.

**What Freemasonry actually is.**

Beneath the myths is something considerably less dramatic: men meeting regularly under a shared code of conduct, using ritual and symbol to teach personal integrity, practising charity, and supporting one another. It survives on precisely the qualities the myths overlook: consistency, discretion and an absence of spectacle. The reality is quieter than the fiction, and that is largely why the fiction persists.
